# Influences

This game openly takes inspiration from **Fallout**, **Cyberpunk** (the genre and *Cyberpunk 2077*) and **Blade Runner**. This doc names what we learn from each, where it shows up, and what we **don't** copy.

**Ground rule:** genres, themes and techniques are free for everyone to use. Specific names, characters, logos, catchphrases and signature designs belong to their owners. We take the *how*, never the *what*.

---

## Which influence goes where

Mixing three styles everywhere would feel messy. Instead, **each influence leads in a different kind of place**. Where you are tells you which one you're feeling.

| Place | Lead influence | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Fulcrum Station**, Lumenhold spaces | Cyberpunk / Blade Runner | Dense, layered, lit by Lumenhold ads, with a rich-and-poor divide by deck |
| **Ruins, Rimefall, wrecks, Old World relics** | Fallout | The aftermath of disaster, told through what was left behind |
| **Faction homes, frontier planets** | Our own "medieval space" | Guilds, shrines, stone and timber, oaths |
| **The Blink** | Blade Runner | Endless dusk; only neon and ads stay lit |

---

## Fallout

**Parallels already in the lore:**
- A lost older civilization known only through relics → **the Old World**.
- A cheerful company that profits from disaster → **Lumenhold** (compare Vault-Tec).
- Factions offering different answers to a broken world → **Hearthfleet, Meridians, Unlit**.
- Scholars hoarding old knowledge → **the Vesper Order**.

**What we take:**
- **Environmental storytelling:** places tell stories without dialogue. A frozen Meridian lab in Rimefall with the last log entry still on the slate. A wreck in Umbra where the crew's notes stop mid-sentence. These become **Ship's Log clues**. *(Keep it at the content-maturity level we're aiming for: implied, not gory.)*
- **Satire through cheerful propaganda:** Lumenhold posters and station announcements that are upbeat while the truth is grim. This is how the game gets dark humor.
- **Choices in gray areas** where every faction is partly right.

**What we don't copy:** vaults, Pip-Boys, the 1950s retro look, "War never changes", the Brotherhood, Nuka-Cola, or anything else that's specifically Fallout.

---

## Cyberpunk

**Parallels already in the lore:**
- A company more powerful than any government → **Lumenhold** (compare Arasaka).
- Gig work and middlemen → **War Contracts** and the job board.
- "High tech, low life": amazing ships, and everyone in debt.

**What we take:**
- **Brokers ("fixers"):** human middlemen who hand out War Contracts and take a cut. One lives in the bazaar (see LORE.md → Characters). It puts a face on the job board.
- **A vertical class divide on Fulcrum Station:** Lumenhold offices and clean upper decks above, crowded lower decks and the bazaar below. You see who the system serves just by walking around.
- **Corporate branding everywhere:** "Light for All" on gates, ships, contracts and ads.
- *(Optional)* **Dusklens fitted as eye implants** when a pilot signs their contract. The debt is literally in your eyes. This is darker, so it's up to you.

**What we don't copy:** Night City, specific corporations, the "netrunner" vocabulary, chrome-and-neon street gangs, or *Cyberpunk 2077* characters.

---

## Blade Runner

**Parallels already in the lore:**
- A powerful company that controls the truth → **Lumenhold** and the **Almanac** (compare Tyrell).
- Permanent dusk → **the Blink**, which is getting longer.

**What we take:**
- **The Blink as a mood:** when the Flame goes dark, Fulcrum Station is lit only by ads and signs. It should be the game's signature visual moment.
- **Noir investigation:** Chapter 4 (*The Almanac*) is a detective story. The player gathers clues, questions people who might be lying, and follows a trail to an uncomfortable truth.
- **Manufactured memory:** in Blade Runner, memories can be implanted. Here, **the Almanac is the world's implanted memory.** Lumenhold has rewritten history for over a century, and nobody knows what really happened.
- **"What counts as alive?"** Prior Anselm suspects the stormdust was *made*. The next question is whether it *thinks*. If the Ion Storm is aware, tending its sun alone for over a thousand years, then Lumenhold's harvesting isn't mining. It's harming a living thing. *(Open decision, see LORE.md.)*
- **One quiet, devastating speech:** Blade Runner's most famous moment is a short monologue, not a fight. Give the story one or two of those, for example Vance's choice, or a message from the Tender.

**What we don't copy:** replicants, the Voight-Kampff test, "tears in rain", Tyrell or Wallace, or the rainy-Los-Angeles setting.

---

## What all three teach

1. **Show the system through ordinary people.** The story's power comes from a debtor, a dockworker, a quartermaster, not from speeches about corporations.
2. **Let the setting tell the story.** Places, posters and leftovers say more than exposition.
3. **Hope is small and personal.** Nobody topples the megacorp. Someone makes one brave choice, and it matters.
4. **Style serves the theme.** The neon, ruins and dusk exist to make players *feel* the story, not just look cool.
