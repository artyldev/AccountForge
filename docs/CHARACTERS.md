# Characters

Full backstories and story arcs for the major characters, mapped onto the chapters in STORY.md. Names and details are tentative.

---

## How these arcs are built

Each character has:
- **Want:** what they're consciously chasing.
- **Need:** what they actually have to realize or accept. **The gap between want and need is their story.**
- **Flaw:** what gets in their way.
- **Secret:** something the player uncovers.
- **Voice:** how they talk, with a sample line.
- **Arc:** what happens to them in each chapter.

**How arcs work in an MMO:**
- **Big turning points ship as chapter updates, and each player sees them at their own point in the story.** A player still in Chapter 3 finds Vance at the shipyard; a player past Chapter 4 finds his replacement. No live events are needed.
- **Player choices change how a character treats *you*** (dialogue, trust, rewards, epilogue), not the shared world.
- **Where the story could go either way**, the author picks the canon outcome. Player choices change dialogue, rewards and epilogues. Shared outcomes decided by players can come later, if there's a player base.

## Relationship web

- **Vance and Idris:** Vance grew up in the Rimeborn camps. Idris's ancestor caused Rimefall.
- **Vance and Tally:** they co-signed contracts years ago, when Tally was an Assessor. Vance is the one who eventually recognizes Tally.
- **Crane and Anselm:** teacher and student. Her leaving the Vesper Order was his greatest grief.
- **Juno and Idris:** Juno secretly buys Titan Hearts for him.
- **Juno and the Ceasefire:** she brokered the contract that broke it, without knowing what it was.
- **Kettering and the Emberwrights:** the other half of her own faction.
- **Wren and Rook:** she works for him. He's half a father figure, and won't admit it.
- **Wren and Grandmother Ise:** an elder of the Listeners at the Altar. Wren left her to become a Guide.
- **Rook and the Hollow Fleet:** its flagship is his old ship.
- **Tally and the player:** Tally holds the original version of the player's contract.

## Quartermaster Vance
**Lumenhold, runs the shipyard on Fulcrum Station.** Mid-fifties.

**Background:** born in the Rimeborn refugee camps on Solace. At sixteen, a Lumenhold recruiter offered him an apprenticeship contract at the shipyard, which was the only way out anyone had ever offered him. He's spent 35 years working his way up to quartermaster. He still sends half his pay to his sister in the camps, and he's still paying off his own Ledger.

- **Want:** give new pilots the start he got. He genuinely believes Lumenhold did right by him.
- **Need:** see that the system that rescued him is the same one that keeps the camps full.
- **Flaw:** gratitude that has turned into loyalty he doesn't question. He'd rather not know.
- **Secret:** since about Year 360, orders for "export hull components" have tripled. He has never asked where they go.
- **Voice:** gruff and warm, practical, talks in ship terms.
  > *"Everybody owes the Hold, kid. What matters is what you do while you're paying."*
  > *"I don't ask where the parts go. I ask if the welds hold."*

**Arc:**
- **Ch 1:** your mentor. He hands you your ship, explains the Ledger kindly, and does your first repair at cost.
- **Ch 2:** you bring back salvage from Umbra, and he recognizes a serial number on a Hollowed hull. It's a ship he built. He goes quiet.
- **Ch 3:** a rush order of export components. He asks you to escort a sealed shipment that "nobody's supposed to see." You can look inside: the parts match Titan plating. He refuses to look.
- **Ch 4:** Kettering's evidence and yours finally reach him. He opens the manifests himself: the components are Titan parts, built in his shipyard. He copies the manifests and leaks them. **After this chapter:** Lumenhold has dismissed him.
- **Ch 5+:** he goes home to Solace and becomes the Rimeborn's engineer. He builds the parts for relighting the Damper's anchors. He has to work alongside Idris Vale, whose family name his family has cursed for a century.

**Per-player variation:** how he says goodbye to you in Ch 4 depends on whether you helped him find the truth or judged him for not looking sooner.

## Almanac-Keeper Edda Crane
**Lumenhold, head of the Almanac office.** About sixty.

**Background:** she studied at the Vesper Order under Prior Anselm and was the best student he ever had. She left because the Order "kept knowledge in a box nobody opened"; she wanted numbers to matter to real people. She became Almanac-Keeper in **Year 381**, the year the Shard War began. On her first day, her predecessor showed her two sets of Blink records, the true one and the published one, and explained that the truth would end the world faster than the dark. In the middle of a new war, she believed it.

- **Want:** keep the peace and prevent panic.
- **Need:** trust people with the truth, and accept that her kindness is a form of control.
- **Flaw:** she's certain she knows better than everyone else.
- **Secret:** **the Second Almanac**, her private, handwritten record of the true Blink lengths, kept for 31 years.
- **Voice:** precise, quiet, never raises her voice. Speaks in numbers.
  > *"A number is only dangerous in the wrong hands. I've simply been very careful about hands."*

**Arc:**
- **Ch 1:** no face, only her signature on every Almanac page and her voice on the Station's Blink announcements.
- **Ch 2:** she presides over **Almanac Day**, the yearly release ceremony on Fulcrum Station. For now it's a short story scene; it could become a live ceremony if the game grows.
- **Ch 3:** Prior Anselm's measurements of the storm's drift reach her. She visits Vesper in secret, and players can spot her ship there. Overheard: *"You taught me numbers were sacred." / "I taught you they were true."*
- **Ch 4:** the target of the detective story. Players piece together the Hearthfleet bell-records, Juno's contract records and Anselm's measurements, and find the Second Almanac. Following the Blade Runner lesson that action comes before words, **she unlocks the archive for you first, then gives her quiet speech** (see INFLUENCES.md), then hands you the book.
- **Ch 5+:** **after this chapter:** Lumenhold "retires" her "for health reasons." She escapes to Vesper. Her mathematics is what makes calculating the Ion Storm's true course possible. She redeems herself through work, not speeches.

## Warden-Captain Maud Kettering
**Hearthfleet, commander of the Cinder Wardens.** Early fifties.

**Background:** she's descended from the guild-master who led the Rekindling around Year 210, the act that set Cinderreach burning forever. The Kettering name is still spoken quietly in Hearthfleet halls. Her family sent her to the Wardens at fifteen as penance, and she rose to Captain.

- **Want:** atone by guarding the fire until it goes out, and keep everyone else away from it.
- **Need:** stop treating guilt as her whole identity. The fire isn't hers to carry alone.
- **Flaw:** rigid, distrustful, quietly proud of her own suffering.
- **Secret:** the Hearthfleet council cut the Wardens' funding to pay for the war, under pressure from the Emberwrights. She's been paying Warden Hunt bounties out of her own savings, and selling family heirlooms to do it.
- **Voice:** blunt, few words, a soldier's way of speaking.
  > *"My family lit that fire. I'll be the one who watches it till it's out."*

**Arc:**
- **Ch 1:** posts the Warden Hunts and leads your first Titan fight. Ends with: *"Rogue machines don't mine this neatly."*
- **Ch 2:** in the background, the Wardens keep watching the Titans.
- **Ch 3:** she asks you to tag a loaded Titan with a tracker. The signal goes dark right on a Lumenhold courier lane. Together with the "couriers are never attacked" pattern, she has her case.
- **Ch 4:** her evidence plus Vance's manifests prove the Titans are Lumenhold's. She takes it to the Hearthfleet council, and the **Emberwrights want to seize the Titans' mining site for war production** instead of shutting it down. Her own faction betrays what she stands for.
- **Ch 5+:** the Hearthfleet's internal split comes to a head over Cinderreach. Your reputation with the Wardens and the Emberwrights shapes who leads. Her growth: she lets others share the weight, the player included. Her last scene is a visit to Carillon's bell-towers on Threnody: looking at what the Hearthfleet was, not only at what it did.

## Chief Engineer Idris Vale
**Meridians, head of research.** About forty.

**Background:** the Vale name carries Rimefall. An ancestor, **Chief Engineer Corwin Vale**, signed off on the shard drain around Year 300. The family spent generations in disgrace, and Idris rebuilt the name through sheer brilliance, becoming Chief Engineer young. He keeps Corwin's original calculations framed in his office, "to remember the error." Privately, he believes Corwin's math was right and the materials were wrong.

- **Want:** a power source no one can take away. Break the monopoly, and redeem the Vale name by finishing what Corwin started, correctly this time.
- **Need:** accept that some limits can't be engineered around, and listen to the Rimeborn, the people who paid for his family's pride.
- **Flaw:** pride and impatience. He sees people as variables.
- **Secret:** he's secretly buying Titan Hearts through Juno Sallow, to build a **Heart engine** at a hidden lab on **Pallas**.
- **Voice:** fast, brilliant, charming, always in a hurry.
  > *"Every great thing was called reckless the day before it worked."*

**Arc:**
- **Ch 1:** in the background: a standing offer for Titan Hearts at the bazaar, through Juno.
- **Ch 2:** he hires you to deliver a sealed crate (Hearts) to Pallas. First meeting: charming, full of talk about freedom from the Hold.
- **Ch 3:** he hears Anselm's finding that the storm is alive and waves it off: *"Everything's alive if you squint."* He wants a stormdust sample.
- **Ch 4:** after the reveal, the Meridian council turns to him, and he speeds up the Heart engine. He meets Vance, and it's tense.
- **Ch 5+:** **the engine test**, the chapter's climax. Your choices with the Rimeborn and the Vale circle decide how it plays out for you: it runs with safeguards, or it fails and threatens a new Rimefall that you have to help contain. Either way, his turning point is choosing to shut it down himself, at personal cost, and saying publicly what Corwin never did: that he was wrong.

## Wren
**Unlit, a Guide.** About nineteen.

**Background:** born in Umbra into a family of Listeners. Her grandmother, **Ise**, is an elder at the Altar. At fifteen, Wren left the Altar to guide salvagers into the dark, and her family took it as betrayal. She works for Rook and flies by sound and Truesight.

- **Want:** prove the Unlit aren't giving up, and be useful to the world outside.
- **Need:** accept that her family's tradition holds truth too. Listening isn't surrender.
- **Flaw:** reckless, eager to prove herself, impatient with elders.
- **Secret:** she's started to hear the Quiet, the very thing she ran away from, and it's getting clearer.
- **Voice:** quick, dry humor. Says very little in the dark.
  > *"Out here you don't look. You listen."*

**Arc:**
- **Ch 1:** cameo, selling salvage at the bazaar and mentioning Umbra.
- **Ch 2:** your companion. She guides you into Umbra, you earn Truesight, fight the Hollow Fleet and visit the Altar. Ends with: *"Every battle out there sends us more of these. The war is feeding it."*
- **Ch 3:** when the Ion Storm passes near Umbra, the Quiet *flinches*. She notices, and sends word to Anselm through you.
- **Ch 4:** after the reveal, the Unlit split into Listeners and Guides. Her grandmother asks her to come home to the Altar.
- **Ch 5+:** the Altar turns out to be the Damper, a harness, and that hits her hardest: her family's holy place was built to hold the Quiet back. **The Maw speaks once, through her** (see below). She backs relighting the anchors, *"not caging it, holding it,"* and reconciles with Ise, who may not agree. She becomes a new kind of Listener: one who listens *and* acts.

## Guildmaster Rook Halloran
**Umbra Salvage Guild.** Mid-fifties.

**Background:** he flew War Contracts for both sides for a decade. In Year 393 he lost his whole crew near Sable, in a battle nobody won. He took his payout and founded the Salvage Guild: *"If the war's going to leave wrecks, someone might as well earn from them."*

- **Want:** profit, and his crew's safety.
- **Need:** stop treating the war like weather, something you profit from but can't change.
- **Flaw:** cynicism. He hoards information.
- **Secret:** his **drift map**: 19 years of records of wrecks drifting into Umbra, and the Hollow Fleet's size after every big battle. It's proof the war feeds the Maw. He's never shared it, because it would bring Lumenhold down on his business, and because it would mean admitting his old crew's ship might be out there, Hollowed.
- **Voice:** wry, tired, gallows humor.
  > *"Every battle's a paycheck for me. Doesn't mean I have to like who's signing it."*

**Arc:**
- **Ch 2:** hires you. You find the drift map, and he shares part of it.
- **Ch 3:** in the background, the wrecks keep piling up.
- **Ch 4:** he publishes the drift map alongside the Almanac reveal, or you take it from him (per-player choice).
- **Ch 5+:** the Hollow Fleet's flagship turns out to be ***the Second Wind*, his old ship.** He asks you to bring it down "properly." After that, he stops hoarding, and his salvage crews carry anchor parts into Umbra for the restoration project.

## Juno Sallow
**Independent broker in the bazaar.** Mid-thirties.

**Background:** her parents were bazaar traders, ruined by Lumenhold rent increases. The lesson she took: never be on a side, be the bridge. She became the best War Contract broker on Fulcrum Station.

- **Want:** stay neutral, and get rich enough that Lumenhold can never ruin her the way it ruined her parents.
- **Need:** realize that neutrality in a rigged game is a side: the house's.
- **Flaw:** self-interest dressed up as being practical.
- **Secret:** in **Year 397**, as a young broker, she handled an anonymous contract, "escort interdiction near Tallow," paying triple. It was the job that destroyed the Hearthfleet convoy and broke the Ceasefire of Sable. She's suspected the truth ever since, and kept the record. She's also Idris's Titan Heart buyer.
- **Voice:** fast, warm, always making a deal.
  > *"I don't pick sides, love. I pick percentages."*

**Arc:**
- **Ch 1:** introduces you to War Contracts.
- **Ch 2:** steady work.
- **Ch 3:** she shows you a pattern: contract bonuses swing between the two sides month to month, always keeping them even. It's the first clue the war is being managed.
- **Ch 4:** her Year 397 record is a key piece of the detective story. Confessing it costs her everything. You choose whether to pressure her or protect her.
- **Ch 5+:** for the first time in her life, she picks a side, and puts her fortune behind the restoration project or the Ledgerbreakers.

## Tally
**Ledgerbreakers.** Age and face unknown. Uses they/them in public; nobody knows more.

**Background (secret):** a former Lumenhold **Assessor**, a debt collector, and once the best in the Ledger Office. During a collection, Tally noticed that the debtor's contract in the Almanac's legal archive had different terms from the copy the debtor had signed. The terms had been changed after signing. Tally found hundreds more, reported it, and was told to forget it. Instead, Tally disappeared, went off the Almanac feed, and became the Ledgerbreakers' best-known voice.

- **Want:** free everyone from the Ledger.
- **Need:** learn that tearing down without building leaves people with nothing. Raids hurt couriers who are debtors too.
- **Flaw:** zealotry. The ends justify the means.
- **Secret:** a former Assessor, and **Tally holds the original version of the player's own contract.** The hidden clause the player finds at payoff was added after signing.
- **Voice:** heard only through glitches in the player's Lens HUD. Cold and sharp.
  > *"They didn't sell you a ship. They sold you a leash with an engine on it."*

**Arc:**
- **Ch 1:** rumors, and graffiti in the bazaar.
- **Ch 2:** first contact. Your HUD glitches, and a message arrives from off the feed.
- **Ch 3:** asks you to help raid a Lumenhold stormdust-harvest convoy. You can help, refuse, or warn the convoy.
- **Ch 4:** provides evidence of altered contracts. When you pay off your Ledger, your hidden clause links back to Tally.
- **Ch 5+:** the Ledgerbreakers escalate after the reveal. Tally has to choose between joining the restoration project (building) or continuing to burn. Vance recognizes Tally as the Assessor he once co-signed contracts with.

## Prior Anselm
**Vesper Order, its elder.** About eighty-five.

**Background:** he's lived on Vesper for sixty years. The Order is down to a dozen members. He taught Crane, his brightest student, and her leaving was his greatest grief. The Order keeps fragments of the Keepers of the Course's writings, including the steps of *the Tending*, which no one can interpret.

- **Want:** read HELIOS before the Order dies out.
- **Need:** share what the Order knows. **The Order is repeating the Keepers' mistake**, guarding knowledge until it becomes ritual, and then nothing.
- **Flaw:** secretive and slow, afraid of Lumenhold.
- **Secret:** the Keepers' texts on the Tending.
- **Voice:** gentle. He answers questions with better questions.
  > *"We aren't keepers of answers. We're keepers of better questions."*

**Arc:**
- **Ch 3:** shows you that the storm is alive, and shows you the shepherd grains.
- **Ch 4:** reunites with Crane, and helps make sense of the Second Almanac.
- **Ch 5+:** with Crane's mathematics, he decodes the Tending: the ritual steps were real instructions for guiding the storm. He matches HELIOS on the shepherd grains to the Altar. **He opens the Order's archive to everyone.** He may not live to see the restoration finished, and passes the work to Crane and the player.

## Non-human characters

### The Ion Storm (the Tender)
- **What it is:** a living swarm, guided by the Old World for a thousand years, and alone since the Keepers forgot it.
- **How it communicates:** patterns of light. In Ch 5, the Lens can begin to translate them, using the decoded Tending.
- **Its one moment** (the second quiet speech, late in Ch 5). Draft, in our own words:
  > *"I was here before your bells. I watched your sails cross the light, more times than you have names for. I watched them burn. I have circled the dark ever since, looking for the path. I do not remember the way. You do. Show me."*

### The Maw (the Quiet)
- **What it is:** a natural, aware, black-hole-like being. Hungry, not evil.
- **How it communicates:** only through Unlit who have learned to listen, and in Ch 5, through Wren.
- **Its one moment.** It reframes relighting the anchors as a mercy rather than a prison. Draft:
  > *"Always hungry. Never full. Once, something held me, and the hunger was quiet. Then it let go. Hold me again."*

### The Lumenhold Board
- **Faceless by design.** Players never meet a single villain to defeat. They meet the system: the Almanac, the announcements, the contracts.
- If a face is ever needed, keep it to one figure, **the Chair**, seen only on broadcast screens.
