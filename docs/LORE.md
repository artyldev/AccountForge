---

## Writing rules

These keep the story free of plot holes. Check new lore against them.

1. **Every lore claim explains something players can see:** the Blink, the standard gear, the dark regions, the varied planets, bosses that come back. If a claim explains nothing in the game, cut it.
2. **Powers have limits and costs.** What a power *can't* do matters more than what it can.
3. **No pure heroes or villains.** Each faction is right about something and wrong about something.
4. **Keep power and knowledge apart.** No single group gets both. That's where conflict comes from.
5. **Lore never depends on one specific mechanic.** Write "weapons run on flame energy", not "ammo" or "heat", so gameplay can change without a retcon.
6. **New terms are taught by the game.** Show the thing before naming it. Explain a term in the same sentence it first appears.
7. **Mysteries are clearly marked as mysteries.** The author knows every answer except the deliberate permanent ones.
8. **Old World links to Earth stay ambiguous.** Many hints, no confirmation. No single hint proves it on its own.
9. **Tone: the One Piece approach.** Take heavy themes seriously (debt, war profiteering, control of information, refugees) but show them through specific people, imply violence instead of showing it, and always leave room for hope.

## How the world works

- **The Elder Flame** is the sun. In the Firstlight it was whole. The **Great Flare** was a natural disaster that tore pieces off it and flung them across the sky. Nobody caused it.
- **Every planetoid formed around a shard.** The shard gives a planetoid its gravity, its atmosphere and its environment. A burning shard makes a lava world. A drained shard makes an ice world.
- **Space is deadly.** Away from a shard's pull there is no air and no warmth, which is why every ship is fully enclosed.
- **Shard energy is flame energy.** Ships, weapons, lights and settlements all run on it. Shards can be used up, and a spent shard never comes back.
- **Shards are still bound to the Elder Flame.** Burning one drains the Flame a little. *(Almost nobody knows this.)*
- **The Seam and the Blink.** Once a day the Flame passes through the Seam: it enters at one horizon, goes dark while inside, and comes out at the opposite horizon. Old records say the Blink was once instant. **It is getting longer.**
- **The Maw** lives in the Seam. It is aware, ancient and patient, but it is **not a god**. It feeds on the light that leaks from the wound. It is weak in light, so it can only reach into the world during the Blink and in the deep dark of Umbra. **It grows as the Flame weakens.** The Hearthfleet call it "the Maw", the Meridians "the Null Point", the Unlit "the Quiet".
- **Hollows** come out of the Seam during the Blink. They contain no flame essence at all.
- **The Ion Storm** is a leftover of the Great Flare: a planet-sized cloud of charged flame-matter that was thrown off and never settled. It drifts between worlds. Its outer cloud is a wall of lightning. In its calm eye, the lightning has fused rare ore over centuries.

### Vision
- **Dusklens:** standard-issue lenses made by the Consortium from crushed low-grade shard glass. Because the glass is impure, they show only one color.
- **Truesight:** a lens cut from a *pure* shard, a craft only the Unlit know. **It doesn't see light. It senses flame essence**, and everything near a shard is soaked in it: rock, people, ships. That's why it works in total darkness, and in full color.
  - **Limit:** Hollows contain no flame essence, so Truesight can't see them.

## The Old World

Before the Firstlight there was another civilization. It left behind relics, broken machines and the **Titans**. Nobody knows where it came from or what happened to it.

### What players find
- **Titans:** vessels the size of cities, built in a style nothing like Consortium work: smoother, older, strange. Most are dead hulks. A few still move.
- **The Unknown Signal:** every active Titan broadcasts the same repeating transmission. Nobody can decode it.
- **Titan Hearts:** the power cores of Titans. **They aren't shards.** No one knows how they work.
- **HELIOS:** a word stamped on Titan hulls and on the Lumen Consortium's oldest seal. Nobody can translate it.
- **Old names:** a few planetoids, **Pallas** and **Vesper**, have names that don't come from any known language. They were already called that in the oldest Firstlight records.
- **Relics:** a gold disc engraved with a diagram of lines and dots, a coin with a face and unreadable letters, star charts with one circled star that isn't in the sky.

### Hints at Earth (never confirmed)
Pallas is a real asteroid in our solar system. Vesper is an old name for Venus as the evening star. Helios is the Greek sun god. The gold disc nods to the Voyager Golden Record. **None of this is ever confirmed in the game.** Players who know will notice, and everyone else just sees mysterious relics.

### Why Titans keep coming back (boss respawns)
Titans **repair themselves.** Destroy one and its systems slowly rebuild it, including a new Titan Heart. That explains respawning bosses and repeated Titan Heart drops. Titan Hearts **can be taken but not made**: only a Titan can build one.

## Timeline

1. **The Old World:** unknown. Only relics and Titans remain.
2. **The Arrival** *(hidden, see Hidden truths)*
3. **The Firstlight:** the Elder Flame is whole. A long age of prosperity.
4. **The Great Flare:** the Flame breaks. Shards scatter, planetoids form, the Seam opens, the first Blink happens. Records are lost.
5. **The Scattering:** survivors are stranded on separate planetoids. They name many of their new worlds out of grief: Threnody, Mourn, Ossuary, Marrow.
6. **The Founding:** a guild of engineers builds the first sealed ships and reconnects the worlds. They are hailed as saviors and become the **Lumen Consortium**. They build **the Station** as neutral ground for trade.
7. **The Divergence:** people disagree about how to live in the broken world, and the three ways of life form.
8. **The Disasters:** Cinderreach, Rimefall and Umbra, one per faction.
9. **Present day:** the **Shard War** between the Hearthfleet and the Meridians. The Consortium supplies both sides. The Blink keeps getting longer.

### Where planet names come from (flavor)
- **Survivor names** (grief and hope, from the Scattering): Threnody, Mourn, Marrow, Ossuary, Harrow, Sable, Solace, Halcyon, Rookery, Quill, Kestrel.
- **Consortium names** (industrial, given to worlds they developed): Gantry, Ferrous, Bellow, Basalt, Tallow, Verge, Lement, Cinder, Umber.
- **Old World names** (no one knows their origin): Pallas, Vesper.

## Power and knowledge

| Group | Power | Knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| **Lumen Consortium** | All of it: ships, weapons, trade, the Station, the Almanac | Only what it stole (see Hidden truths) |
| **The Cartographers** | Almost none | The most anyone has about the Old World |
| **Hearthfleet / Meridians / Unlit** | Territory and people | Each knows one piece of the truth |

### The Lumen Consortium (tentative name)
**Public face:** the company that saved the world. It builds every ship, weapon and pair of Dusklens. It owns the Station, its shipyard and markets, the job boards and the currency. It publishes the **Flame Almanac**, the official record of Blink lengths. It accepts payment in shards.

**How players feel its influence:** everywhere and quietly. Prices rise during the Blink "for your safety." Jobs for both sides of the war are posted on the same board. Even independent traders at the bazaar pay the Consortium rent. There is no cackling-villain moment. The evil is in the contracts.

**Human faces:** many Consortium employees are decent people who believe in it.

**Real-world model:** the East India Company.

### The Cartographers (tentative name)
A small, secretive order that maps old routes, collects relics and tries to decode the Unknown Signal. They have knowledge but no power: no fleet, no territory. The Consortium **quietly suppresses** them. Officially they're "cranks and relic thieves." They lead the Old World storyline and are a small quest-giver group, **not** a full reputation faction.

## The three ways of life

All three fly the same Consortium ships and carry the same weapons. They differ in **beliefs, cities, clothing, banners and ship colors** (cosmetic only).

### The Hearthfleet: the old ways
Guild families bound by oaths and crafts. They live in stone-and-timber holds and keep shrines to the Elder Flame. They believe the Flame will recover if people stop burning so many shards.
- **Right:** they've guessed the real cause.
- **Wrong:** they still fly shard-burning ships to fight for their beliefs.
- **Their disaster: Cinderreach.**

### The Meridians: the future
Engineers and planners who build ivory-and-gold cities lit with solid light. They want a power source of their own to break the Consortium's monopoly, and they'd kill for a Titan Heart they could understand.
- **Right:** the monopoly is the real danger.
- **Wrong:** their shortcuts cost whole worlds.
- **Their disaster: Rimefall.**
- *Renamed from "Meridian Concord" so the name can't be confused with "Consortium."*

### The Unlit: something else
They live in and around Umbra, study the Maw, and believe the dark is coming whatever anyone does, so they've learned to live in it. They are the makers of Truesight.
- **Right:** the dark can be understood and survived.
- **Wrong:** they've stopped trying to save anyone else.
- **Their disaster: Umbra.**

### Region stories

**The Station:** built by the Consortium at the Founding as neutral ground. Its company stores (the mini markets) sell at fixed Consortium prices. The **auction house** is Consortium-run and takes a cut of every sale. The **bazaar** is where independent traders sell, and they pay the Consortium rent. The **shipyard** builds every ship in the system. The **social area** is the one place where everyone, from every side of the war, drinks at the same bar.

**Cinderreach:** the Hearthfleet tried a **Rekindling**: they fed a great shard to "give light back" to the Flame. It burned out of control and never stopped. Hearthfleet forge clans harvest its heat at great risk. **The Titan** roams here, drawn to the burning shard's heat to recharge. Its Unknown Signal is how most pilots first find it.

**Rimefall:** the Meridians drained a great shard to build a power source of their own. The shard died, the region froze, and the atmosphere thinned. Frozen ruins of a Meridian citadel are still locked in the ice. Survivors' families are still refugees.

**Umbra:** once kept lit by old shard-beacons. The Unlit put the beacons out because they burned shards. The region went dark, and the Maw drew closer. It lies nearest the Seam, so the Blink is felt strongest here.

## The player's start

The player starts **on the Station** with a Consortium ship **and the contract that comes with it**. That's not unusual. **Every pilot's first ship comes on a Consortium contract.** It's simply how anyone gets a ship, and a ship is how anyone makes a living. Most people are still paying decades later. Background NPCs talk about family debts passed down, but the player's own debt is the ordinary kind everyone has.

This is the modern-debt theme (needing loans to get a start in life), handled through the system rather than one sad backstory. See GAME_DESIGN.md → The Ledger.

## Hidden truths (author only)

1. **The Consortium has known for generations that shards are bound to the Flame**, and that every shard burned dims it. They hide this by altering the Flame Almanac.
2. **Their goal isn't to fix the Flame.** A healed Flame means free light, and that would end their business. The war burns shards, which weakens the Flame, which makes their stockpile worth more. When the Flame is dim enough, **the Consortium owns the last light.**
3. **The Consortium didn't invent sealed ships.** Its founders **stripped them from a dead Titan** and claimed the invention. HELIOS on their seal was copied from that Titan's hull. They took the ship designs, but never understood the rest: the Hearts, the Signal.
4. **Titan Hearts are miniature suns**, an Old World technology. **They don't drain the Elder Flame.** If anyone learns to build them, the Consortium's monopoly ends and the Flame could be spared. That's why the Consortium suppresses the Cartographers, and it's the long-term thread of hope.
5. **The Old World** *(author's private answer, never confirmed in the game)*: the people's ancestors arrived long ago aboard Titan-escorted ships, an expedition named HELIOS. Over the centuries of the Firstlight they forgot, and the Great Flare destroyed the last records. The Unknown Signal is the expedition's old call sign, still repeating.
6. **The central irony:** everyone fighting over the Flame is burning it. The Old World knew how to make suns, and the present world is burning its only one.
7. **Every faction has blood on its hands** (Cinderreach, Rimefall, Umbra). The Consortium's crimes come after the Great Flare: hiding the truth, stealing the ships, profiting from the war.
