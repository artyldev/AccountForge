# Lore Bible

Working draft. Anything marked **(tentative)** is not final. Anything in **Hidden truths** is for the author only. Players uncover it slowly, or never.

---

## Writing rules

These keep the story free of plot holes. Check new lore against them.

1. **Every lore claim explains something players can see.** The Blink, the standard gear, the dark regions, the varied planets. If a claim explains nothing in the game, cut it.
2. **Powers have limits and costs.** What shard energy *can't* do matters more than what it can.
3. **No pure heroes or villains.** Each faction is right about something and wrong about something.
4. **Lore never depends on one specific mechanic.** Write "weapons run on flame energy", not "ammo" or "heat", so gameplay can change without a retcon.
5. **New terms are taught by the game.** Show the thing before naming it. Explain a term in the same sentence it first appears. Early on, introduce no more than one new term per conversation.
6. **Mysteries are clearly marked as mysteries.** Players should know when they aren't supposed to understand something yet. The author knows every answer except the deliberate permanent mysteries.
7. **Tone: the One Piece approach.** Take heavy themes seriously (debt, war profiteering, control of information) but show them through specific people, imply violence instead of showing it, and always leave room for hope.

## How the world works

- **The Elder Flame** is the sun. In the Firstlight it was whole. The **Great Flare** was a natural disaster that tore pieces off it and flung them across the sky. Nobody caused it.
- **Every planetoid formed around a shard.** The shard gives a planetoid its gravity, its atmosphere and its environment. A burning shard makes a lava world. A drained shard makes an ice world. That's why there are about 21 very different planetoids.
- **Space is deadly.** Away from a shard's pull there is no air and no warmth, which is why every ship is fully enclosed.
- **Shard energy is flame energy.** Ships, weapons, lights and settlements all run on it. Shards can be used up, and a spent shard never comes back. Low-grade shards give weak, dirty energy. Pure shards are rare.
- **Shards are still bound to the Elder Flame.** Burning one drains the Flame a little. *(Almost nobody knows this. See Hidden truths.)*
- **The Seam and the Blink.** The Great Flare left a wound in the sky. Once a day the Flame passes through it: it enters at one horizon, goes dark while inside, and comes out at the opposite horizon. Old records say the Blink was once instant. **It is getting longer.**
- **The Maw** is something that lives in the Seam. It is aware, ancient and patient, but it is **not a god**. It feeds on the light that leaks from the wound. It is weak in light, so it can only reach into the world during the Blink and in the deep dark of Umbra. **It grows as the Flame weakens.** Each faction has its own name for it: the Hearthfleet say "the Maw", the Concord say "the Null Point", the Unlit say "the Quiet".
- **Hollows** are creatures that come out of the Seam during the Blink. They contain no flame essence at all.

### Vision
- **Dusklens:** standard-issue lenses made by the Consortium from crushed low-grade shard glass. Because the glass is impure, they show only one color.
- **Truesight:** a lens cut from a *pure* shard, a craft only the Unlit know. **It doesn't see light. It senses flame essence**, and everything near a shard is soaked in it: rock, people, ships. That's why it works in total darkness, and in full color.
  - **Limit:** Hollows contain no flame essence, so **Truesight can't see them**. The upgrade makes you powerful, not safe.

### The Old World
Every so often, relics turn up from a civilization older than the Firstlight: objects nobody can explain. The oldest seal of the Lumen Consortium bears a word no one can translate: **HELIOS**.
- These relics are **easter eggs and atmosphere, never plot**. The story never depends on them.
- Whether the Old World is *our* world is **never confirmed.**

## Timeline

1. **The Old World:** unknown. Only relics remain.
2. **The Firstlight:** the Elder Flame is whole. One sky, one people.
3. **The Great Flare:** the Flame breaks. Shards scatter, planetoids form, the Seam opens, the first Blink happens.
4. **The Scattering:** survivors are stranded on separate planetoids with no way to travel between them.
5. **The Founding:** a guild of engineers works out how to harness shards and builds the first sealed ships. They reconnect the worlds and are hailed as saviors. They become the **Lumen Consortium**. *(They also discover that shards are still bound to the Flame, and they hide it.)*
6. **The Divergence:** people disagree about how to live in the broken world, and the three ways of life form.
7. **The Disasters:** Cinderreach, Rimefall and Umbra, one per faction (see Regions).
8. **Present day:** the **Shard War** between the Hearthfleet and the Concord over the remaining shard fields. The Consortium supplies both sides. The Blink keeps getting longer.

## The Lumen Consortium (tentative name)

**Public face:** the company that saved the world. It builds every ship, weapon and pair of Dusklens. It runs the shipyards, the job boards, the clinics and the currency. It publishes the **Flame Almanac**, the official record of Blink lengths. It accepts payment in shards.

**How players feel its influence:** everywhere and quietly. Every shop and job board is the Consortium's. Prices rise during the Blink "for your safety." Jobs for **both sides of the war** are posted on the same board. Planetoids have Consortium company towns. There is no cackling-villain moment. The evil is in the contracts.

**Human faces:** many Consortium employees are decent people who believe in it. The rot is in the system, not in every person.

**Real-world model:** the East India Company, a trading company with its own army that ended up ruling a subcontinent.

**What it wants:** see Hidden truths.

## The three ways of life

All three fly the same Consortium ships and carry the same weapons. They differ in **beliefs, cities, clothing, banners and ship colors** (cosmetic only).

### The Hearthfleet: the old ways
Guild families bound by oaths and crafts. They live in stone-and-timber holds and keep shrines to the Elder Flame. They believe the Flame will recover if people stop burning so many shards.
- **Right:** they've guessed the real cause.
- **Wrong:** they still fly shard-burning ships to fight for their beliefs. Hypocrites, and they know it.
- **Their disaster: Cinderreach.**

### The Meridian Concord: the future
Engineers and planners. They build ivory-and-gold cities lit with solid light. They want a power source of their own to break the Consortium's monopoly.
- **Right:** the monopoly is the real danger.
- **Wrong:** their shortcuts cost whole worlds.
- **Their disaster: Rimefall.**

### The Unlit: something else
They live near the Seam, study the Maw, and believe the dark is coming whatever anyone does, so they've learned to live in it. They are the makers of Truesight.
- **Right:** the dark can be understood and survived.
- **Wrong:** they've stopped trying to save anyone else.
- **Their disaster: Umbra.**

## Regions

About 21 planetoids, roughly 3 per region. Each planetoid's environment is shaped by the shard inside it. Its terrain loosely follows one of Roblox's built-in terrain materials (check the current material list in Studio).

| Region | Status | Theme | Terrain |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Umbra** | exists | darkness, the Maw, the Unlit | Slate · Rock · Mud |
| **Rimefall** | exists | extreme cold, Concord ruins | Snow · Ice · Glacier |
| **Cinderreach** | exists | extreme heat, Hearthfleet forge clans | CrackedLava · Basalt · Ground |
| **Hearthhaven** | proposed | Hearthfleet homeland | WoodPlanks · Cobblestone · Brick |
| **Meridian** | proposed | Concord capital | Concrete · Pavement · Asphalt |
| **The Greenwake** | proposed | wild frontier, Consortium farm colonies | Grass · LeafyGrass · Limestone |
| **The Glass Shoals** | proposed | glassed sands, Firstlight ruins, Old World relics | Sand · Sandstone · Salt |

### Cinderreach: the Hearthfleet's disaster
The Hearthfleet tried a **Rekindling**: they fed a great shard to "give light back" to the Flame. It burned out of control and never stopped. Today Hearthfleet forge clans harvest its heat at great risk, and the Concord wants to take it over.

### Rimefall: the Concord's disaster
The Concord drained a great shard to build a power source of their own. The shard died, the region froze, and the atmosphere thinned. Frozen ruins of a Concord citadel are still locked in the ice. Survivors' families are still refugees.

### Umbra: the Unlit's disaster
Umbra was once kept lit by old shard-beacons. The Unlit put the beacons out because they burned shards. The region went dark, and the Maw drew closer. It lies nearest the Seam, so the Blink is felt strongest here. Without Truesight, travelers depend on their Dusklens.

## The player's start

The player begins with a Consortium ship **and the debt that comes with it** (see GAME_DESIGN.md → The Ledger). The player is part of the system from day one: their ship burns shards like everyone else's.

**Open question:** did the player sign the contract, or inherit it? An inherited debt (passed down from family) fits the tone, but it's a heavier theme.

## Hidden truths (author only)

1. **The Consortium has known for generations that shards are bound to the Flame**, and that every shard burned dims it. They hide this by publishing the Flame Almanac with altered Blink records.
2. **Their goal isn't to fix the Flame.** A healed Flame means free light, and that would end their business. Their plan:
   1. Sell ships and weapons to every side, paid in shards.
   2. The war burns shards, so the Flame weakens faster.
   3. A weaker Flame makes shards worth more, and so their stockpile is worth more.
   4. When the Flame is dim enough, **the Consortium owns the last light.** Everyone pays them or freezes.
3. **The central irony:** everyone fighting over the Flame is burning it, using weapons made from it.
4. **Every faction has blood on its hands** (Cinderreach, Rimefall, Umbra). The Consortium's crime isn't the Great Flare, which was natural. Its crime is everything it did afterward.
